# Is This Job Offer Real? — Job Scam First Aid

**Operation Help Humanity · Mission 02 · AiNUNNAKI**

Looking for remote work? A fake recruiter or a convincing onboarding portal can put money or sensitive data at risk. **You never need to share the offer or personal information with this project.**

**Important:** These are warning signs, not a definitive scam detector. Some genuine jobs involve remote interviews. Verify the employer independently; don't assume a name, logo, or company-looking website proves an offer is genuine.

## Six warning signs

1. **Pay to work or get paid.** A recruiter asks for a deposit, paid “training,” crypto, gift cards, or a payment to unlock earnings.
2. **Equipment-check scam.** The employer sends a check and asks you to buy equipment or send money to its “vendor.” Even a check that appears cleared can later be reversed.
3. **Unexpected easy-money pitch.** Unsolicited texts or messages promise large remote-work pay without a clear role.
4. **Clicking/task scams.** A fake app pays you to “optimize” products or rate content, then demands crypto to release fictitious earnings.
5. **Sensitive data too early.** Someone requests your Social Security number, bank account login, ID image, or one-time security codes through an unverified channel.
6. **Pressure or secrecy.** They insist you must act immediately, won't let you verify, or demand you move to a private messaging app.

## Verify before accepting

- Find the company's official website and careers page yourself. Check the exact job title and location.
- Contact the company using independently verified contact details, **not** only those from the offer.
- Search the company and recruiter alongside “scam,” “complaint,” and “review.” No reported complaints doesn't guarantee safety.
- Slow down. Ask someone you trust to review the situation.
- Don't send money to someone claiming to be a recruiter, and don't rely on a deposited check to buy company equipment.

## If you've already sent money or information

- **Money or check:** Contact the bank or payment service immediately through its official channel and ask about stopping/disputing the payment. Explain if you deposited a suspicious check. Refunds are not guaranteed.
- **Password or security code:** Change it through the real service, end other sessions if possible, and enable multifactor authentication.
- **Identity details:** In the U.S., go to [IdentityTheft.gov](https://www.identitytheft.gov/) for a tailored recovery plan and contact impacted institutions.
- **Report suspected fraud:** U.S. reports can go to [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/) or [FBI IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/). People in other countries should contact appropriate local authorities.
