Scam First Aid

A suspicious message? Sent money or shared a password? Here are practical first steps. You don't need to paste the message, upload documents, or tell us anything personal.

No AI diagnosis · No tracking · No submitted data
Important: No checklist can prove that a message is safe or fraudulent. When in doubt, contact the organization using its official app, a known bookmark, or a number on your statement/card—not contact details from the suspicious message. If someone is in immediate physical danger, contact local emergency services.

Looking for work? See our companion Job Scam First Aid guide covering fake recruiters and remote-work scams.

Before you click

Urgency and threats

“Pay in 10 minutes or your account closes” is a reason to slow down, not rush.

Unexpected requests

Be careful with surprise links, attachments, passwords, security codes, or requests to move money.

Familiar names can be faked

A logo, caller ID, or apparent sender name does not by itself establish authenticity.

Safest next move: Open the service separately using a trusted method and verify the claim there. Do not reply to the suspicious message to “check” it.

What happened? Choose the closest situation.

Open an item for steps. These are general recommendations, not case-specific financial, legal, or technical advice.

I received a suspicious email or text, but didn't interact.
  1. Avoid its links, attachments, and listed contact numbers.
  2. Verify separately through the organization's official site or app if the subject is important.
  3. Use your messaging service's spam/report feature, then delete or archive it.

In the U.S., you may also report the scam to the FTC. FTC guidance includes forwarding suspicious texts to 7726 (SPAM).

I clicked a link or opened a suspicious attachment.
  1. Stop interacting with the page or file. Do not enter passwords, codes, or payment details.
  2. Update your device's security software and run a scan. If software was installed or remote access was granted, seek trusted technical support.
  3. If you entered a password, change it through the official service from a trusted device and enable multifactor authentication.
  4. Review the affected accounts for unrecognized activity.

See the FTC's response guide.

I sent money or disclosed card or bank information.
  1. Contact your bank, card issuer, or payment service immediately through its official support channel.
  2. Tell them what happened; ask about stopping the payment, recovering funds, and protecting the account. Available options depend on payment method and timing.
  3. Save receipts and relevant evidence without sharing passwords or full account numbers publicly.
  4. In the U.S., report the incident through ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

See the FTC's payment-method-specific steps. Recovery cannot be guaranteed.

I shared an account password or a one-time login code.
  1. Visit the official service directly, change your password and sign out of other sessions where supported.
  2. Turn on multifactor authentication; check account recovery information and linked devices.
  3. Secure the email account connected to that service, and update reused passwords elsewhere.
  4. Check for unauthorized transactions, messages, and changes.

CISA: turn on multifactor authentication.

I disclosed sensitive identity information or suspect identity theft.
  1. Contact any affected financial or government institution through its official channel.
  2. In the U.S., visit the official FTC IdentityTheft.gov service for situation-specific recovery steps.
  3. Follow guidance appropriate to what was exposed. Consider credit protections where indicated by your recovery plan.

Outside the U.S., consult your country's official consumer protection or identity theft reporting service.
