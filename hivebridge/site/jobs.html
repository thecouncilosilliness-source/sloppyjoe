Mission 02 · Practical protection

Is this job offer real?

Scammers sometimes impersonate recruiters, post fake remote-work openings, or create convincing onboarding websites. This guide helps you slow down and verify an offer without exposing your personal information.

Free · No registration · No personal information collected
No automatic verdict: An unfamiliar recruiter or chat interview is not proof of fraud, and an employer-looking webpage isn't proof of safety. Verify each opportunity through an independently located official company contact. Do not send us the offer or personal documents.

Red flags worth stopping for

Pay to get paid

A supposed employer asks for a deposit, training fee, cryptocurrency, gift cards, or a payment to unlock commissions.

Equipment check trick

You receive a check to buy equipment and are told to send money to a designated vendor. An apparent deposited or “cleared” check can still turn out to be fake.

Unsolicited offers

Unexpected texts or WhatsApp/Telegram messages promise easy remote work, big pay, and little information about the job.

Fake tasks

“Product boosting,” clicking, or rating tasks show imaginary earnings before demanding your money to continue or withdraw them.

Too much data, too soon

A person demands your Social Security number, banking credentials, government ID, or verification codes through an unverified onboarding link.

Pressure and impersonation

Time limits, demands for secrecy, or a familiar brand in the sender name can be tactics; logos and caller ID can be forged.

A safer verification process

  1. Stop replying for now. Don't click fresh links, download files, deposit checks, share codes, or send money to test the offer.
  2. Find the company independently. Search for its established official website yourself and look for a careers page listing the specific role.
  3. Contact the company through a verified channel. Ask if the recruiter, role, and interview method are genuine. Don't rely on phone numbers or links supplied solely in the suspicious message.
  4. Research the details. Search the company/recruiter name together with “scam,” “complaint,” or “review.” An absence of complaints does not guarantee legitimacy.
  5. Take your time. Discuss the offer with someone you trust. Legitimate hiring should not depend on paying a stranger to unlock your wages.

If you've already acted

I shared a password or one-time security code

Go directly to the official account service from a trusted device, change passwords, end active sessions where possible, and enable multifactor authentication. Secure any connected email account. See our Scam First Aid guide.

I paid a recruiter, used cryptocurrency, or deposited a check

Stop sending funds. Contact your bank or payment provider promptly through their official channel, explain the situation, and ask about stopping or disputing the transaction. If you deposited a check, explain the circumstances to the bank before moving or spending those funds. Recovery depends on method and timing; no refund is guaranteed. See the FTC payment-specific recovery guide.

I sent identification, a Social Security number, or bank information

Contact affected institutions and seek official, situation-specific identity recovery guidance at IdentityTheft.gov (United States). Do not post identifying documents on social media or in open-source project issues.

I want to report the fake job

U.S. users can report suspected scams at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. Internet-enabled crimes may also be reported to the FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center. Outside the U.S., use your country's consumer or cybercrime reporting service. Do not post personal contact information here.
